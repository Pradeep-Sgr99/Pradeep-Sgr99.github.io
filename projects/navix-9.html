NAVIX-9

Precision dual-GNSS RTK navigation & inertial reference unit
Role: Hardware design & PCB layout Company: OSEC MCU: STM32H743VIT6 (Cortex-M7) Revision: Rev 8 (finalized BOM) Board: Multilayer mixed-signal PCB

Overview

NAVIX-9 is a precision navigation board that fuses two u-blox ZED-F9P RTK GNSS receivers with a dual-IMU inertial suite to produce centimetre-level position and GNSS-derived heading. An STM32H743 Cortex-M7 runs a 100 Hz Extended Kalman Filter over the sensor set and manages isolated industrial I/O (CAN FD, RS-232), wired Ethernet, environmental sensing and microSD data logging. The design targets moving-platform navigation where a single GNSS antenna cannot resolve orientation.

Microcontroller & system

  • STM32H743VIT6, Arm Cortex-M7 with double-precision FPU — headroom for the 100 Hz EKF plus concurrent GNSS / RTCM stream handling.
  • Debug: 2-wire SWD (SWDIO / SWCLK / NRST); the BOOT0 strap selects Flash vs. the system bootloader (UART / USB DFU).
  • Clocks: HSE crystal for the system clock; 32.768 kHz LSE crystal for the RTC.
  • Rails: the core LDO decoupling caps (2.2 µF to ground) are placed close to the MCU.

Positioning — dual u-blox ZED-F9P

Each module exposes two UARTs to the MCU: UART1 for the NMEA/UBX host link and UART2 for the RTCM3 correction stream. Routing both RTCM paths through the MCU (rather than module-to-module) gives firmware full visibility and control of the correction data.

Bus allocation: I2C2 is unusable while Ethernet is populated — its pins are committed to the RMII interface. That leaves three hardware I2C buses for four devices, so the two timing-critical IMUs each get a dedicated bus and the two low-rate sensors share one bus at distinct addresses.

Storage, indication & misc

  • microSD (MSD-4-A) in SPI mode on SPI4, with a mechanical card-detect line. On-board logging of raw GNSS, IMU and fused output.
  • RGB status LED — three GPIO channels (moved off analog-only pins in an earlier revision).
  • Buzzer — GPIO or timer PWM for tone control.
  • Backup domain — VBAT coin cell keeps the RTC / backup registers alive across power loss.

Firmware architecture

  • 100 Hz Extended Kalman Filter fusing both IMUs, RTK position and moving-baseline heading into a single navigation solution.
  • Correction handling: RTCM3 stream from each ZED-F9P is passed through the MCU, so firmware can monitor, filter or re-source corrections rather than relying on a fixed module-to-module link.
  • Dead-reckoning: the automotive-grade ASM330LHHX carries the solution through GNSS dropouts.
  • Time base: module PPS / TIMEPULSE lines are wired to the MCU for GNSS-disciplined timing of the filter and logs.

Design notes & revision history

  • Rev 8 — IMU #2 changed from BMI085 (Bosch, dual-die) to ASM330LHHX (ST, single-die, AEC-Q100). Same I2C4 bus and interrupt lines — relabelled only, no layout change.
  • Rev 7 — Ethernet reverted from a W5500 SPI controller to LAN8742A / RMII (internal MAC); the freed SPI bus was repurposed for GNSS #1 control lines. Both IMUs moved from SPI to I2C.
  • The CAN transceiver standby (STB) is actively driven — its internal pull-up otherwise defaults to standby.
  • GNSS #1 control reuses pins that also carry JTAG functions — fine because debug stays in 2-wire SWD.
  • Final netlist confirmed in STM32CubeMX before layout — particularly the I2C bus split and the dual-GNSS UART2 routing.
